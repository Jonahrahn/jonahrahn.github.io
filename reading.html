§ Reading · A working bibliography

What I read to think clearly.

A working bibliography. The books, papers, and tools that shaped how I think about language and the systems we build on top of it. Not a canonical list; just the things I keep returning to.

Books — Linguistics

  • Speech and Language Processing Jurafsky & Martin

    The reference text. Worth re-reading the chapters on dialogue and semantics every few years; they age better than the chapters on neural methods. The free online drafts are good.

  • Linguistic Fundamentals for Natural Language Processing Bender & Lascarides

    The crash course every ML engineer who works on language should take. Short, opinionated, and ruthless about why ignoring linguistic structure costs you accuracy.

  • The Atoms of Language Mark Baker

    A defense of the parameter-setting view of language variation, written for non-linguists. Whether or not you buy the theory, the argument changes how you look at cross-linguistic data.

  • The Language Instinct Steven Pinker

    My on-ramp into linguistics. Aged unevenly but still the clearest popular case for why language is a structured cognitive system, not a learned cultural artifact.

Books — Machine Learning

  • Neural Network Methods for NLP Yoav Goldberg

    Pre-transformer in its examples but the mental model of NLP-as-structured-prediction holds up. Read it for the framing, not the architectures.

  • Foundations of Statistical Natural Language Processing Manning & Schütze

    Old, but the chapters on information theory and on collocations are still the best treatment of those topics anywhere.

  • Probabilistic Machine Learning Kevin Murphy

    When I need the proper Bayesian treatment of something, I open Murphy. Two volumes, both free online.

Papers I return to

  • Climbing towards NLU Bender & Koller, 2020

    The clearest articulation of the form-meaning distinction. Required reading before discussing what LLMs do or don't understand.

  • Stochastic Parrots Bender, Gebru, et al., 2021

    Independent of one's politics about it, the methodology section is a good audit checklist for any large language project.

  • BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers Devlin et al., 2019

    The transition point. Worth reading alongside the original transformer paper to see how the field went from "attention is a useful trick" to "attention is the whole architecture."

  • Subword Neural Machine Translation Sennrich, Haddow, Birch, 2016

    BPE in its original framing. Re-read whenever a tokenization decision is in question.

  • The Annotation Of Coreference Pradhan et al., OntoNotes guidelines

    Not a paper exactly. A 200-page document that taught me more about what "consistency" means in practice than any textbook chapter.

Tools I reach for

  • spaCy

    For most production NLP plumbing, this is the first thing I install. Opinionated, fast, well-maintained.

  • Praat

    Phonetic analysis. Forty years old, still unmatched for visualizing spectrograms and pitch contours.

  • HuggingFace Transformers + Datasets

    The default place to fetch models and the default place to publish benchmarks. Has flaws; still the right starting point.

  • Label Studio / Doccano

    The two annotation platforms I keep coming back to. Label Studio for span work and adjudication; Doccano for quick projects.

  • Universal Dependencies

    The closest thing the field has to a shared syntactic vocabulary across languages. Imperfect, indispensable.

Newsletters & ongoing reading

  • Sebastian Ruder's NLP newsletter
  • Jay Alammar — visual explanations of transformer internals
  • Lilian Weng's blog — surveys of where the field is
  • Language Log — University of Pennsylvania linguistics blog
  • ACL Anthology — the firehose
